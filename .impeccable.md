## Design Context

### Users

BNS WebUI serves three overlapping audiences:

- Visitors who need to search a BNS name, resolve a DID, inspect ownership, documents, and public activity without connecting a wallet.
- Wallet users who register and manage names, publish documents, renew, transfer, and follow a transaction from wallet approval through chain confirmation to indexer convergence.
- Advanced owners and controllers who understand authority paths, guards, document versions, hashes, controller policies, and atomic mutations.

The primary job is to make protocol state and authority understandable enough that a user can act safely. The interface must always distinguish asset ownership from effective authority, and chain success from query projection completion.

### Brand Personality

Trustworthy, precise, and composed. BNS should feel like durable public infrastructure with a human-facing registry, not a speculative crypto marketplace. The product voice is candid about protocol limitations, calm during long-running transactions, and explicit around irreversible actions.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined “network registry” visual language: warm, lightly tinted surfaces; dark ink-like typography; crisp rule lines; restrained protocol diagrams; and a single confident accent family. The interface should feel closer to a well-designed atlas or records office than a generic admin dashboard. Information density can be high, but hierarchy and whitespace must keep it legible.

Support light and dark themes, with light as the default. Preserve a visual relationship to the BuckyOS family without copying its desktop shell. Avoid NFT-marketplace tropes, token-price visuals, neon-on-black crypto styling, glassmorphism-heavy layouts, and decorative gradients.

### Design Principles

1. State before spectacle: raw chain/indexer facts, derived labels, and uncertainty are visibly separated.
2. Progressive disclosure: public lookup and common wallet tasks stay simple; authority, bytes, hashes, and batch tooling live behind advanced affordances.
3. Safe by construction: high-risk actions show before/after state, the exact network and Proxy, and an explicit final confirmation.
4. Two-stage transactions are a first-class object: wallet, chain, and projection phases form one continuous, recoverable experience.
5. Dense does not mean cramped: use strong type hierarchy, aligned data rows, and varied spacing instead of wrapping every value in a card.
